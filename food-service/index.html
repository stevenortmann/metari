Why kitchens

Food is where humanoids still struggle most.

Soft and slippery food, hot surfaces, sharp tools, wet floors and people moving fast. Simulation struggles to model it, and a real restaurant cannot stop service for a test. Labs need real kitchen data. Operators need proof before anything touches a shift.

01 · HARD DATA

Contact-rich, messy, real.

Scooping, wiping, plating and racking: the manipulation labs most want and can least get.

02 · NO DISRUPTION

Never during service.

Training and testing happen in Metari replicas, so no restaurant pauses a shift and no guest sees a test.

03 · SAFETY FIRST

Rules before reach.

Hot zones, knife zones, allergen separation and person-in-reach holds are written into every task before an attempt runs.

How it works

From your best cooks to a humanoid on the line.

The people who already do the work well are the experts. Metari turns how they do it into data humanoid labs can train on, then measures every humanoid against it.

01

Pick the station

Your kitchen team picks the tasks, the variations (menu items, plate types, rush volume, layout) and what a good plate or a clean station looks like.

02

Learn from your cooks

Experienced chefs, cooks, dishwashers and servers do the work in a Metari replica kitchen, on camera. Ceiling, wall and wearable views share one clock.

03

Train

Humanoid labs train their own models on those expert demonstrations and on corrections from each attempt.

04

Validate

Every humanoid runs the same tasks, including variations it has not seen, under food-safety and person-in-reach guardrails.

05

Deploy and verify

Passing tasks move into supervised service in selected kitchens, starting with off-peak and closing work. Evidence returns to one record.

Your cooks are the experts

Know-how that usually walks out the door, captured once.

Cooks come to a Metari replica of your kitchen for scheduled sessions. They do the work the way they do it on a good night, then correct the humanoid when it gets it wrong. Those corrections are some of the most valuable data a lab can get.

  • Paid, scheduled sessions outside service
  • Consent for every recording
  • No guests, and no food served to the public
  • Rights to each episode agreed with you up front
What you receive

One scorecard. Every task, every vendor.

Each attempt is scored against the acceptance criteria you wrote, across the same set of variations. Interventions are logged, and every result links back to the recorded episode.

Path to deploy

Start small. Scale what the evidence supports.

Each phase is scoped and agreed before it starts. Nothing moves into a working kitchen until it has passed in the replica.

01

Task discovery

Rank prep, dish, front-of-house and closing tasks by labor hours and fit. No changes in your kitchens.

02

Replica validation

Your cooks demonstrate the top tasks, and humanoids run them in Metari’s kitchens.

03

Supervised kitchen validation

Passing tasks move into a few kitchens, starting with off-peak and closing work, with staff supervising.

04

Group rollout

Expand task by task and location by location. Each new kitchen feeds the next round.
