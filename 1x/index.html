Metari × 1X · Partnership concept

Stop collecting what happens. Produce what you need.

Dedicated environments that produce the exact task, in the exact setting, as many times as you need it, then become the places operators come to see NEO work: hotels, hospitals, senior living, student housing and homes.

The fit

NEO has to work in real buildings. We can build any of them on demand.

1X starts in the home, and has been open that the path runs through human video before it runs through robot data. But the tasks NEO learns there are the same tasks that run a hotel, a care home, a hospital ward and a student block. The constraint is environmental, not a shortage of footage. You already own the capture hardware and the model.

What is hard to buy is supply: the right rooms, the right tasks and the right people, running continuously and on instruction.

A hotel room is a bedroom, a bathroom and a wardrobe. Behind it sit kitchens, laundries, corridors and storerooms. The work is bed-making, folding, wiping, stacking, carrying and loading. Deformables and clutter, the class of task that resists simulation hardest.

Metari is led from inside the industry, on a twenty-year executive hospitality career running and opening properties at multi-property scale, with the relationships to source operating hotels for conversion and to identify sites for acquisition.

Those relationships put thousands of rooms within reach. Capacity is added by taking floors out of service and instrumenting them, not by constructing buildings, so the ceiling on this programme is how fast rooms can be fitted out, not how much real estate exists.

A hotel room is the only commercial real estate already shaped like a home, and the only kind you can lease by the floor and rebuild at will into a patient room, a senior-living suite, a dorm or an apartment.
The shift

From reactive capture to proactive capture

Every wearable programme in occupied buildings is reactive: it records whatever happened to occur that day. A dedicated environment inverts that. Your team names the scenario and we produce it, on schedule, to volume.

Stage 01

Collect

Dedicated rooms, trained hospitality staff, and the same task run again and again with only the variables your team asked us to change.

Humans first, room second

Every run starts with a person. Trained hospitality staff wear your capture rigs and perform the task the way it is actually done on shift, not a staged demonstration and not a gig worker improvising in an unfamiliar space.

The room adds a second layer on top: fixed cameras in the ceiling, in every corner and on the walls at working height, time-synchronised and calibrated into one coordinate frame with the worn capture.

Ground truth from outside the wearer

The fixed layer records where objects actually were, what the whole body was doing, and a second view of any moment the head-mounted view lost.

Because the room never moves between the demonstration and the trial, the humanoid’s attempt can be measured against the human’s in the same coordinates. That comparison is what this slider stands in for.

Repetition is the product

Knock the lamp over. Leave the towels on the floor. Change the linen, drop the lighting, swap the person doing the work. Re-run it. Then run it ninety more times.

When a task list arrives on Monday, collection against it starts the same week, not whenever a matching situation happens to turn up in someone's house.

The environment library

Every space in an operating property, available as a capture set.

  • Consent cleanNo guests and no guest belongings. Everyone on camera is staff, working under a capture agreement written for this purpose.
  • JurisdictionalCleaning standards, chemicals and innkeeper law differ by state and by country. Sites in different markets produce genuinely different data, not more of the same.
  • Edge casesSpills, breakages, biohazard protocol and maintenance faults can be staged deliberately. That is the long tail a live-building programme will almost never catch on camera.
  • WorkforceCapture is run by trained hospitality staff on a shift pattern, recruited and supervised by Metari. Sites in the United States and Latin America let a programme scale hours without waiting on a single labour market.
  • Chain of custodyStorage, transfer, retention and deletion run to your specification, with a per-batch manifest of what was captured, by whom, in which configuration, and under which consent.
Reconfigurable

One shell. Every building NEO will eventually work in.

A guest room is a bare box with plumbing, power and a door. Change the furniture and the fittings and it becomes a hospital room, a senior-living suite, a dorm, an apartment or a short-term rental, while the camera rig and its calibration stay exactly where they are. That rig is the expensive part, and it never moves.

Most of these are markets where real-world capture is effectively impossible, because you cannot film inside an occupied care home, a working ward or a student's room. A mock environment is the only way that data exists at all.

The same argument runs forward into deployment. A humanoid proven on hospitality tasks is a humanoid proven on the tasks all of these buildings share: bed-making, laundry, tidying, resupply and cleaning between occupants. Each configuration is both a training set and a market NEO can be sold into.

Stage 02

Train & evaluate

Collection that stops at delivery is a one-off purchase. The same site, held still and run as an evaluation environment, is where the partnership compounds year over year.

Step 1

Humans wearing your rigs

Trained hospitality staff wear 1X capture rigs and do the real job at working speed, while the room's fixed cameras record the same run.

Step 2

Model training

Your team trains against the delivered set. We hold the environment exactly as it was.

Step 3

Humanoid trials

The policy runs the same task, in the same room, against the same acceptance criteria.

Step 4

Recreate the failure

Whatever broke gets rebuilt on demand and captured again, targeted at the gap.

A benchmark you can hold still

Evaluating a policy needs the opposite of a diverse dataset: an environment that does not drift. Same room, same props, same starting state, measured the same way each time, so a change in the score means a change in the model.

Every batch ships with QA metrics rather than a claim: tracking success rate, keypoint error, share of frames passing review, and the calibration state of every camera in the room.

Stage 03

Deploy

The same building that produced the data becomes the room where a family decides they want one at home, where a hotel group signs for two hundred, and where a health system or a senior living operator watches one work a patient room before risking it on a real floor.

Consumer adoption needs a room, not an ad

A household spending twenty thousand dollars on a humanoid wants to stand next to one first. A campus with a public-facing floor does what a video cannot: a counter, a demonstration home, and staff who will answer honestly about what broke last month.

It also produces something useful: a continuous record of how untrained members of the public actually behave around the robot.

Every sector buys the same way: on site

A hotel group, a senior living operator, a hospital system or a student housing owner does not want a slide deck. They want to watch one make a bed, walk the back of house, and question the team running it. One campus serves all of them, because the rooms reconfigure to whatever sector is visiting that week.

Metari brings the property side: operator and ownership relationships across the western United States and Latin America, used both to source hotels for conversion and to reach the groups who would buy a deployment. That is the part of the route to market that is slowest to build and hardest to buy.

The long game

One campus proves it. A network absorbs the fleet.

As NEO production scales, the constraint moves from building robots to placing them somewhere useful, keeping them running, and proving they work in each new setting. A site network answers all three at once.

Horizon 2

Horizon 3

Portfolio deployment

Units move from campus into the properties those campuses have been selling to: hotels, senior living, healthcare, student housing and residential, with the same team supporting them on the ground.

  • SustainmentEvery campus carries a service bay: charging and diagnostic docks sized to the fleet, technicians trained by your team, and a local parts and covering inventory. Units cycle rather than queue.
  • Wear dataContinuous operation on real tasks surfaces failure modes a lab never sees. That maintenance record is worth as much to your hardware team as the capture is to pretraining.
  • Property strategyThe model starts with leased floors inside operating hotels, moves to whole buildings held for the purpose, and acquires sites where volume justifies it. Each step buys more control over configuration, hours and access, and none of it requires a robot to be in the building first.
  • Room supplyThe properties within reach hold room inventory in the thousands, across multiple markets and property classes. A programme can widen from a floor to a building to several sites without renegotiating who Metari is or how the capture works.
  • Soft landingEach new site is a rehearsal for deployment in that market: local staff trained, local conditions measured, local buyers already through the door.
