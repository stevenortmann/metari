Hotels are the commercial setting shaped most like a home: bedrooms, bathrooms, laundries and service corridors, run to written standards by people who do the work every day. Metari proposes to make those spaces commissionable for micro1.

Proposed by Metari

Domain execution you can commission

  • Configurable environments: guest rooms, laundry, service and supply spaces, set up and reset to an agreed recipe.
  • Industry-led operators: supervised hospitality staff who do the task the way it is done on shift.
  • Auditable variation: layout, lighting and clutter changed deliberately and logged per attempt.
  • Traceable evidence: operational QA, rework and a manifest in an agreed format for micro1's own review.

Not a proposal to replace micro1's labs, researchers, annotation systems or customer relationships. Any speed, coverage or cost benefit would be measured in a paid pilot, not claimed in advance.

What Metari adds

Four things that are hard to buy by the hour.

01

Rooms on demand

Real guest rooms taken out of service and instrumented. No guests, no guest belongings, no waiting for the right moment to happen.

02

Operators who do the job

Hospitality staff trained to property standards, on a shift pattern, supervised by people who have run these departments.

03

Variation by instruction

Change the layout, the light, the clutter or the operator between runs, then repeat. Every change is logged against the attempt.

04

Evidence you can audit

Preflight checks, reason codes, recapture lineage and a per-batch manifest. Rejects are shown, not hidden.

How it runs

One loop. Each pass returns a sharper brief.

The task is set with the partner. Metari captures the expertise, evaluates against the agreed scorecard, and learns from what broke, in the same room on the same rig, so every pass is comparable to the last.

        Feedback loops

        A bad recording and a struggling robot are different problems.

        Both can create new work. They follow different loops, have different owners and prove different things.

        Loop A · Capture defect

        The recording did not meet the specification.

        1. Preflight QA flags an attempt, for example lost hand/object contact in the side view.
        2. A reviewer inspects the evidence and requests a camera or scene correction.
        3. The operator resets the room, checks the changed setup and captures a new attempt.
        4. The rejected attempt is kept with its lineage. It is never counted toward quota.

        Metari's problem to fix, inside the same order.

        Loop B · Capability gap

        Valid evidence shows an agent struggling with the task.

        1. The model owner evaluates on a separate holdout, not on collection scenes.
        2. A finding names the conditions where the agent fails.
        3. That finding becomes a proposed new brief with targeted variants.
        4. micro1 and the model owner approve it before any collection starts.

        A new order, owned by the people who evaluate the model.

        Bad data is not proof of a bad robot. A rejected recording says nothing about model capability.
        A good recording is not proof of model improvement. That needs its own agreed evaluation.

        A 6 to 8 week program, subject to site access, staffing, equipment, consent and technical agreement.

        • One environment, a few work families
        • A jointly agreed sample, perhaps 50 to 100 episodes
        • Reconciled costs, rejects and turnaround at closeout
        After the pilot

        Reserved operating capacity

        Rooms, stations and operator coverage held for micro1 programs, with booking and cancellation rules agreed in advance.

        • Pays for capacity actually withheld from others
        • Any exclusivity narrow and compensated
        • Metari stays an independent operator

        Design the pilot

        Draft a discussion brief, not a checkout.

        Every field accepts "To be agreed". The output lists what is proposed, what is still unknown and which approvals are missing. It generates no price and asserts no available rooms or rigs.

        Next step

        Bring one difficult physical-world requirement.

        Define the task, evidence and acceptance criteria. Together we can scope the physical environment, operators and collection plan.

        The meeting this should lead to is a technical and commercial scoping session with micro1's robotics lead, a collection-operations owner and a technical evaluator. Questions we would bring:

        • Which requests are currently difficult to fulfil?
        • Is the bottleneck capture, action data, annotation or evaluation?
        • What location and privacy restrictions apply?
        • Who specifies and supplies the hardware?
        • What is the accepted unit and the acceptance window?
        • What would make a repeat order or a reserved zone worthwhile?
