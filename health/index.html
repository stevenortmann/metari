Metari Health

Clinical environments where physical AI learns the work.

Hospitals are among the hardest places for a robot to work, and the hardest places to collect training data. Metari Health builds mock clinical rooms, staffs them with credentialed clinicians and instruments them for capture and evaluation. Real procedures, real standards, no patients.

Task order → mock clinical room → clinician + humanoid → structured data → clinician-scored evaluation → repeat

Why healthcare

The work is physical. The data does not exist.

Clinical work runs on precise sequences, sterile boundaries and rules that cannot be broken. Physical AI teams cannot simply film it inside a working hospital. Metari Health gives them a place where the work can be performed correctly, repeated and measured.

Access

Live hospitals are closed to collection

Privacy, infection control and liability keep cameras and robots out of real clinical spaces.

Standards

"Correct" is defined by clinicians

A tray assembled in the wrong order or a broken sterile field is a failure, even if it looks fine to a non-expert.

Demand

Staffing pressure is pulling robots in

Hospitals want help with logistics, turnover and supply work. Robots need proof they can do it safely first.

How a program runs

The same order-driven model. A clinical rulebook.

Metari Health runs on the Metari Command Center. A team sends a task order; we configure the room, staff it with the right clinicians, capture every attempt and return evidence scored against clinical standards.

01

Task order

Tasks, variations, volume and acceptance criteria.

02

Room configured

Layout, equipment and mannequins set to spec.

03

Clinician sessions

Credentialed staff demonstrate, correct and supervise.

04

Capture

Multi-view video, sensors and task events, synced.

05

Clinical review

Experts label steps, errors and safety events.

06

Delivery

Structured episodes plus an evaluation report.

Evaluation

Scored by the people who do the work.

Clinical leaders define what success means before collection starts. Every attempt is scored the same way, so a team can see whether its system is actually getting better.

SequenceWere steps done in the right order, with nothing skipped?
Sterile boundariesDid anything cross a line it should not have?
Safety eventsNear misses, stops and handoffs back to a human.
Completion and timeWas the task finished to standard, and how long did it take?

Illustrative evaluation categories. Final criteria are set with each buyer and clinical advisors.

Roadmap

Two tracks. One starts without patients.

Track 01 · First

Clinical environments

Mock rooms, credentialed clinicians and clinician-scored evaluation for robotics and physical-AI teams. No patients and no protected health information.

  • Mock OR, patient room and sterile processing
  • Expert demonstrations and corrections
  • Evaluation reports buyers can compare over time
Track 02 · Exploring

Clinical data network

Consented, de-identified procedure and outcomes data from academic medical centers, for diagnostics, device and AI teams. It would run only through institutional agreements, ethics review and patient consent.

  • Institution-licensed, de-identified data
  • Consent collected before any capture
  • Separate governance from Track 01
