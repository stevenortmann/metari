Mission areas

Every program starts with three questions.

Metari Government is organized around the task, not the robot. A program is defined by what a system has to do, how that will be judged, and what a decision-maker receives at the end.

01

What task must the robot perform?

Written as steps, conditions and variations an expert would recognize, in an environment that can be held steady.

02

How will it be evaluated?

Agreed metrics, intervention rules and held-out configurations, set before any collection begins.

03

What evidence will you receive?

Records that link each requirement to the episodes, tests and reviewer decisions behind it.

Offering 01

Mission-specific training data

Expert demonstrations and customer-specified sensor capture, organized around defined tasks, quality checks, and delivery formats.

Offering 02

Robot readiness evaluation

Repeatable physical scenarios with agreed metrics, documented interventions, failure review, and evidence for deployment decisions.

Offering 03

Dedicated program capacity

Future configured space, instrumentation, and operating support under an agreed scope, subject to launch conditions.

Proposed programs. Scope, equipment, personnel, rights, pricing, and availability are agreed before delivery.

Environments

Proposed environments for indoor operations work.

Each environment would be configured around the tasks a program specifies, then held steady so attempts can be compared. Platform neutral: humanoids, mobile manipulators, arms or inspection platforms, depending on the program. Select an area for example tasks and proposed outputs.

Area 01

Facility operations

Cart preparation, supply replenishment, linen handling, room reset and cleaning-tool manipulation.

Area 02

Logistics and material movement

Tote transfers, shelf access, sorting, cart loading and doorway navigation.

Area 03

Maintenance and skilled trades

Tool selection, work-area preparation and component handling on safe training equipment.

Partner-led expansion
Area 04

Inspection and infrastructure

Nonhazardous confined-access and infrastructure mock-ups, with platforms and expertise from qualified partners.

Non-patient clinical logistics and room workflows are covered by Metari Health. No patient data is part of Metari Government.

          Engagement

          Scoped commercial relationships, agreed before launch.

          We are forming scoped commercial relationships for future data production and evaluation. Programs begin only after agreement on facilities, equipment, personnel, data rights, acceptance criteria, and readiness.

          01

          Define the requirement

          The tasks, environment, conditions and evidence the program needs.

          02

          Agree the scope and conditions

          Equipment, personnel, rights, acceptance criteria and launch dependencies.

          03

          Establish the commercial agreement

          A services agreement, a paid work package or a teaming arrangement.

          04

          Launch when ready

          Work starts once the agreed facility and readiness conditions are met.

          Robotics companies, primes and partners

          Conditional teaming or a non-binding LOI

          For teams that need data or evaluation capacity for public-sector applications, we can discuss a conditional teaming agreement or a meaningful non-binding letter of intent that names the intended work and its launch dependencies.

          • Intended tasks and environments
          • Data and evaluation deliverables
          • Launch dependencies on both sides
          Start a teaming discussion
          Agencies and laboratory teams

          A technical conversation first

          Tell us about the program requirement. We will discuss whether the work fits and which procurement path would be appropriate. No letter or commitment is needed to start a conversation.

          • Task and evaluation requirements
          • Environment and safety constraints
          • An appropriate procurement path
